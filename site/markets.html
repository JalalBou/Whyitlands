Who is winning, who is paying.

Our read U.S. integrators are shrinking into profit: UPS lost domestic volume after the Amazon glide-down yet grew adjusted profit 12% on yield, and FedEx grew revenue double digits on price while fuel and trade friction ate its margin. Our read is that the volume they shed is flowing to Amazon's own network, which grew operating income 43% while buying delivery speed, so shippers should expect firm list-rate increases (FedEx +5.9% for 2027) and surcharges from the integrators, and more capable alternatives from marketplace logistics.

Our read Posts are still growing parcels (Poste Italiane +11%, Geopost +8%, Austria and PostNord +9%) but almost none is growing profit: La Poste, PostNord, Austrian Post, CTT and Bnode all report lower operating earnings as letter decline, labour costs and parcel price competition outrun volume. The end of de minimis and new EU import fees are already visible in cross-border flows (PostNL international about -15%, CTT customs, InPost Poland marketplaces), while DHL is the outlier, with Express yield and tight air capacity lifting group EBIT 30%. Our read: scale in out-of-home and pricing power, not volume, now separates winners, and FedEx taking control of InPost confirms lockers are the strategic asset.
