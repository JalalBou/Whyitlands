EU · 30 September 2026

Since July, every parcel pays. From November, it pays twice.

Since 1 July a €3 duty applies to every tariff line in a low-value parcel, and from 1 November a €2 handling fee follows. Volumes into the main EU gateways have roughly halved, and the fight is now over who pays, who carries the liability and who owns the data.

Read the briefing Market Intelligence
Why it lands here
  1. 1
    Since 1 July 2026 the EU charges a flat €3 customs duty per tariff subheading in parcels under €150; on 21 September the Commission set the new handling fee at €2 per item from 1 November.
  2. 2
    The customs reform, adopted by Parliament on 16 September, makes platforms and non-EU sellers the importer, creates an EU Customs Authority in Lille and a single EU Customs Data Hub, with fines of 1% to 6% of annual import value.
  3. 3
    Low-value parcel volumes fell by about half in Belgium and the Netherlands, and Chinese e-commerce air exports to Europe were down 40% year on year in August.
  4. 4
    France, Italy and Romania moved first with national fees; the Netherlands and Belgium backed off for fear of parcels diverting to neighbouring gateways.
UK · 30 September 2026

The £135 import loophole now has a closing date.

Britain will end duty-free entry for low-value parcels only in October 2028, after the United States and the EU have already closed their doors. Until then the UK is the soft landing for direct Chinese parcels, while British exporters face tougher terms in both of their biggest markets.

Read the briefing Market Intelligence
Why it lands here
  1. 1
    The UK will remove its £135 customs relief by October 2028, six months earlier than first planned but more than three years after the United States ended de minimis.
  2. 2
    VAT is already collected at checkout by marketplaces; the reform adds duty, a handling fee per consignment, full tariff classification and fiscal representatives.
  3. 3
    Chancellor John Healey's first Budget on 28 October is the next chance to speed up the timetable, as retailers demand.
  4. 4
    Royal Mail, owned by EP Group, is using universal service reform to shift resources to parcels in a market where Evri passed 1 billion parcels and InPost absorbed Yodel.
North America · 30 September 2026

The $800 free pass is gone. Clearance is now part of the product.

Washington has written the end of de minimis into customs law, rebuilt its tariffs after the Supreme Court ruling and refused to renew the USMCA. Parcels now clear as full imports, flows inside North America have become one-sided, and margin is moving to whoever holds the broker licence and local stock.

Read the briefing Market Intelligence
Why it lands here
  1. 1
    CBP suspended the $800 de minimis exemption for all modes in June and July 2026, and statute repeals it from 1 July 2027.
  2. 2
    Foreign posts can no longer file for mail: only the buyer, owner or their designated licensed broker can use the new postal informal entry up to $2,500.
  3. 3
    After the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs, Washington moved to Section 122, then to Section 301 tariffs of 10% to 12.5%, plus a 50% Section 338 duty on some Canadian goods.
  4. 4
    The USMCA was not renewed on 1 July: it stays in force but now faces a review every year, with cars and Chinese content at the centre.
South America · 30 September 2026

Brazil scraps its 20% tax on parcels under $50.

Brazil has zeroed the federal tax on imports up to US$50 and turned it into law days before the 4 October election, while Mexico taxes Asian parcels harder and Argentina opens up. For carriers and platforms, the next fight is the 2027 consumption tax and the compliance data it will demand.

Read the briefing Market Intelligence
Why it lands here
  1. 1
    Law 15,502, signed on 10 September, confirms a zero federal import tax on purchases up to US$50 via Remessa Conforme; state tax of 17% to 20% still applies.
  2. 2
    Import declarations jumped 74% year on year in August, while industry has taken the exemption to the Supreme Court and warns of job losses.
  3. 3
    Both leading presidential candidates back the zero rate, so the real battleground moves to the CBS and IBS consumption taxes from 2027 and to platform compliance duties.
  4. 4
    Mexico's 33.5% courier rate and 2026 tariffs push Asian volume into local fulfilment, while Argentina's US$3,000 courier and postal cap pulls volume into air parcels.

China · 30 September 2026

Shenzhen hosts APEC as the West closes the small-parcel door.

Washington extended its truce with Beijing but kept the de minimis door shut, while Brussels now charges every small parcel. China's platforms and carriers answer by shipping in bulk and delivering locally, and small sellers pay the transition.

  2. 2

Middle East · 30 September 2026

Saudi Arabia plans 42 logistics hubs. 25 already run.

The Iran war has run since 28 February, a June peace deal collapsed in July, and Washington rejected Tehran's latest offer on 26 September. With Brent above $100 and jet fuel more than doubled, surcharges, reroutes and Gulf gateways now set the price of a cross-border parcel.

Why it lands here
  1. 1
    There is no ceasefire: the June memorandum collapsed in July, the United States blockade of Iranian ports resumed on 15 July, and Iran runs Hormuz as a permit regime.
  2. 2
    Brent traded between $104 and $107 on 28 September, and jet fuel prices are roughly double last year's, feeding integrator fuel surcharges above 25%.
  3. 3
    Gulf air hubs have recovered to about 80% of 2025 traffic, but half of pre-war China to Europe capacity relied on them and Asia to Europe flows now fly more direct.
  4. 4
    Jebel Ali volumes are down 90% and cargo detours via Saudi, Omani and UAE east-coast ports at four to five times the cost.
North Africa · 30 September 2026

Casablanca hosts Africa’s logistics sector twice this year.

Morocco's ports and free zones are drawing traffic and Chinese investment, and Suez is slowly reopening. At the same time Rabat, Cairo, Algiers and Tunis are tightening their rules on imports, so the region is getting easier to route through and harder to sell into.

Read the briefing Market Intelligence
Why it lands here
  1. 1
    Tanger Med handled 11.1 million TEU in 2025 and Nador West Med opens its first container terminal in Q4 2026, giving Morocco two gateways to Europe.
  2. 2
    Suez traffic is recovering in stages: August 2026 revenue rose 56.7%, but daily transits are still well below 2023 levels.
  3. 3
    The EU's maritime ETS is moving relay traffic from Algeciras to Tanger Med, and Brussels wants to tighten the rules.
  4. 4
    Egypt now requires advance cargo data for air freight, while Algeria and Tunisia control imports through approvals and payments.

EU

Europe now charges every parcel. Who absorbs the bill?

What happened
Since 1 July 2026 the EU charges a flat €3 customs duty per tariff subheading in parcels under €150; on 21 September the Commission set the new handling fee at €2 per item from 1 November.
Why
The customs reform, adopted by Parliament on 16 September, makes platforms and non-EU sellers the importer, creates an EU Customs Authority in Lille and a single EU Customs Data Hub, with fines of 1% to 6% of annual import value.
What it means
Europe has not stopped the cheap parcel, it has priced it per tariff line: the volume moves to containers and EU warehouses, and the margin moves to whoever owns the customs data.

Middle East

Hormuz in its eighth month: the parcel pays the fuel bill and the detour

What happened
There is no ceasefire: the June memorandum collapsed in July, the United States blockade of Iranian ports resumed on 15 July, and Iran runs Hormuz as a permit regime.
Why
Brent traded between $104 and $107 on 28 September, and jet fuel prices are roughly double last year's, feeding integrator fuel surcharges above 25%.
What it means
Hormuz is not shut, it is taxed: the parcel pays through jet fuel, war-risk premiums and longer Gulf detours, and the bill will outlast any ceasefire.

North America

America charges every parcel and puts North America's trade pact on a yearly leash

What happened
CBP suspended the $800 de minimis exemption for all modes in June and July 2026, and statute repeals it from 1 July 2027.
Why
Foreign posts can no longer file for mail: only the buyer, owner or their designated licensed broker can use the new postal informal entry up to $2,500.
What it means
In North America the parcel now pays at the border in data as much as in duty, so margin moves to whoever files the entry and holds stock inside the wall.

North Africa

Tangier, Suez and the parcel: North Africa's gateway opens as its borders tighten

What happened
Tanger Med handled 11.1 million TEU in 2025 and Nador West Med opens its first container terminal in Q4 2026, giving Morocco two gateways to Europe.
Why
Suez traffic is recovering in stages: August 2026 revenue rose 56.7%, but daily transits are still well below 2023 levels.
What it means
North Africa is becoming Europe's nearest hub and a harder border at the same time: plan routing around Suez and Tangier, and clear upstream wherever you sell.

South America

Brazil scraps its small-parcel tax before the vote. Latin America splits on the parcel

What happened
Law 15,502, signed on 10 September, confirms a zero federal import tax on purchases up to US$50 via Remessa Conforme; state tax of 17% to 20% still applies.
Why
Import declarations jumped 74% year on year in August, while industry has taken the exemption to the Supreme Court and warns of job losses.
What it means
Brazil's small-parcel tax is gone before the vote, but the real bill for cross-border e-commerce will be written in the 2027 consumption tax and in the compliance data platforms and carriers must now supply.

UK

The UK's £135 gap: British parcels caught between Washington, Brussels and Beijing

What happened
The UK will remove its £135 customs relief by October 2028, six months earlier than first planned but more than three years after the United States ended de minimis.
Why
VAT is already collected at checkout by marketplaces; the reform adds duty, a handling fee per consignment, full tariff classification and fiscal representatives.
What it means
Britain is the last big Western market still open to the duty-free small parcel, and the timing of its closure, not the principle, now decides who gains the volume and the margin.
