Storyline · North Africa

North Africa as Europe's gateway

Europe wants supply chains closer to home and away from contested sea lanes, and Morocco sells itself as the industrial back office on its doorstep while Chinese investors use it as a base to serve European factories. At the same time Rabat, Cairo, Algiers and Tunis tighten their own borders, so the region gets easier to route through and harder to sell into.

Our read

Our reading: Moroccan hubs gain from Suez rerouting and EU carbon rules on sea relay and consolidated B2B loads, but the EU's per-parcel charges make direct consumer parcels from Morocco no cheaper than from Asia. Clearing upstream with duties shown at checkout is the condition for selling into Morocco and Egypt, while Algeria and Tunisia stay approval-led B2B markets.
