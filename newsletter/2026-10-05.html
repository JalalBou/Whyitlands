THE LEAD · TRADE · GLOBAL E-COMMERCE
The small-parcel door closes: who pays, who declares, and where volume goes
The US, the EU, the UK and Japan are ending duty-free treatment for low-value e-commerce parcels, while Brazil has just reopened its door. For carriers and platforms, the question is no longer whether a small parcel pays, but who declares it, on what data and from which warehouse.

EU
Europe now charges every parcel. Who absorbs the bill?
Europe has not stopped the cheap parcel, it has priced it per tariff line: the volume moves to containers and EU warehouses, and the margin moves to whoever owns the customs data.
UK
The UK's £135 gap: British parcels caught between Washington, Brussels and Beijing
Britain is the last big Western market still open to the duty-free small parcel, and the timing of its closure, not the principle, now decides who gains the volume and the margin.
Middle East
Hormuz in its eighth month: the parcel pays the fuel bill and the detour
Hormuz is not shut, it is taxed: the parcel pays through jet fuel, war-risk premiums and longer Gulf detours, and the bill will outlast any ceasefire.
North Africa
Tangier, Suez and the parcel: North Africa's gateway opens as its borders tighten
North Africa is becoming Europe's nearest hub and a harder border at the same time: plan routing around Suez and Tangier, and clear upstream wherever you sell.
North America
America charges every parcel and puts North America's trade pact on a yearly leash
In North America the parcel now pays at the border in data as much as in duty, so margin moves to whoever files the entry and holds stock inside the wall.
South America
Brazil scraps its small-parcel tax before the vote. Latin America splits on the parcel
Brazil's small-parcel tax is gone before the vote, but the real bill for cross-border e-commerce will be written in the 2027 consumption tax and in the compliance data platforms and carriers must now supply.
China
Western doors close to China's parcels, so the platforms move the warehouse
The Western door is closing to the small parcel, not to Chinese goods: the volume is moving into containers and local warehouses, and so is the margin.
North Asia
North Asia moves the parcel tax to the checkout as its delivery capacity runs short
In North Asia the cheap parcel will survive, but it will be taxed at checkout, paid for in scarce delivery hours and carried by whoever holds the data.
Southeast Asia
ASEAN bets on one digital rulebook as every border starts taxing the parcel
Asia's parcels will keep growing, but the value now lies in proving where each one comes from and who collects the tax on it.
South Asia
India opens the door to parcel exports while Washington rewrites the toll
India is lowering the cost of exporting a parcel just as the United States makes each one harder to receive; the winners will be the operators that turn compliance and stock positioning into a product.
Oceania
Oceania keeps its A$1,000 door open while fuel and Washington raise the parcel bill
Oceania's border remains the most open to the small parcel, so in 2026 the bill rises on the road and in the air, not at customs.
